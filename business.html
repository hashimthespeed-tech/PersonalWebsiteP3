The long way around

Try. Learn.
Try better.

No neat overnight-success story here. The useful part has been learning to stay with an idea after the exciting beginning.

01

Seventh grade

A channel, built one upload at a time.

I started a Fortnite YouTube channel and posted every day. It grew to almost 3,000 subscribers, and at one point my videos were getting 5,000–10,000 views. That was my first proof that showing up consistently could reach people.

Fortnite · daily videos · almost 3,000 subscribers
02

Early experiments

I learned what a sale is not.

I discovered dropshipping and barely convinced my parents to let me get Shopify. I didn’t know how to market it, so it didn’t go anywhere. Then I tried Printify phone cases. I sold some to friends, but not to people beyond my circle, and that idea didn’t work out either.

A lot of ambition, not much experience yet
03

A mistake I own

Reselling taught me where my line is.

I tried reselling and bought fake products to sell. I realized I was selling counterfeit items to people, stopped, and paid everyone back because I felt bad about it. I’m not proud of that experiment; refunding the people I sold to was the right thing to do.

Not a win. A lesson I take responsibility for.
04

Trying again

New ideas came faster than follow-through.

After reselling, I tried dropshipping again. I started learning about AI and ChatGPT, especially through social media, and learned a lot, but I didn’t stick with any big project. For the next year or two, I kept chasing whatever seemed like the next big thing. I might try something for a week, hit an obstacle, and decide it didn’t work instead of pushing through. I knew I could communicate well, but I still needed guidance and consistency.

Curiosity was there. Consistency wasn’t yet.
05

Sophomore year and after

Guidance changed how I build.

I joined Super Energy’s class in my sophomore year—not freshman year—and got guidance that helped me get much more into AI. I’ve learned to build more and become a better communicator. While I was learning, I started Fragrance Mog, a fragrance channel. Last year I also started working on an app; I’m still working on it and want to finish it so I can see where it might go.

Learning to make ideas tangible

A summer side project

Door to door.
Day one.

During summer break, I started a bin-cleaning business to make some cash for things like AI subscriptions. I went door to door and got my first customer on the first day.

It was a small start, but it gave me a real reason to ask, listen, and do the work in front of me.

The before-and-after work, not a stock photo.
A real
first step

The end of summer · Bay Area

One week.
One shared goal.

The day after I started my bin-cleaning business, my cousin suggested I come help with his car-detailing business. We had talked about doing it during summer, but by then there was only about a week of break left. I went anyway, even though the ticket cost more than usual.

We set a $1,000 profit goal for the week and reached it. We detailed a lot of cars and knocked on doors, including mansions and homes belonging to people I saw as very successful. My communication improved, and I learned a lot from doing the work alongside my cousin.

The goal was ours for that week. The business is my cousin’s.
